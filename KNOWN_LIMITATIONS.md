# Known Limitations & Archival Transparency
## हमारे नुस्खे / Hamare Nuskhe

---

### 1. Lack of Modern Clinical Validation

Traditional home remedies (*nuskhe*) have accumulated through centuries of empirical household observation, trial, and domestic culinary practice. However:
- The majority of these preparations have **not** been evaluated in rigorous, placebo-controlled, double-blind randomized clinical trials (RCTs).
- Laboratory studies cited in our evidence notes often evaluate isolated phytochemical extracts (e.g. purified curcumin or asiaticoside) at non-culinary concentrations, not the traditional whole-food home preparation.

---

### 2. Status of Entries

- All entries in the public archive without an explicit &quot;Expert Reviewed&quot; stamp carry a status of **DRAFT / Unreviewed**.
- An unreviewed status indicates that while an ethnobotanical or historical citation has been verified, a registered medical doctor or AYUSH practitioner has not yet completed the full clinical contraindication audit.

---

### 3. Individual Biochemical Variability

- Traditional preparations can behave differently depending on a person's age, metabolic state, genetics, kidney/liver clearance, and concurrent pharmaceuticals.
- Plant chemical profiles vary depending on soil composition, harvest season, and storage duration.

---

### 4. Gaps in Classical Botanical Equivalencies

- Some folk plants bear conflicting common names across districts (e.g., *Dodda Pathre* vs *Panikoorka* vs *Ajwain leaf*).
- Wherever botanical taxonomy cannot be confirmed with certainty via Kew POWO or IPNI, `scientificName` is explicitly set to `null` rather than guessing.
